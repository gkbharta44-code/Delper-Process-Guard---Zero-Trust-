1. Purpose and Summary

Delper Aegis sells Delper Guard hardware to factories and charges a recurring subscription for it. Enterprise lines pay a subscription, SME and home users pay smaller recurring fees, and Delper charges a net markup on the FDE staffing that operates each plant. The cloud AI platform and the Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) run monitoring and plant-level operations. The program protects legacy operational technology (OT) from cyber threats while letting AI systems diagnose live plants.

This summary uses the Group summary's economics: the $60,000 enterprise subscription, $10,000 net FDE markup, $6,000 hardware-as-a-service per unit, and the SME and home tiers. The one change from the Group figures is the SME node cost, which is $185 per unit, as decided. All years are program years, with program Year 1 as group Year 7, unless marked as group Year. Section 16 lists the items still to resolve.

2. Product and Architecture

  • Enterprise AI core: a cloud-hosted language model with a vector database of engineering documents, control programs, and incident history.
  • FDE platform: event pipelines, multi-agent orchestration, guardrails against prompt injection, and a service that retrieves data from the hardware.
  • Hardware (Delper Guard): inline deep packet inspection of Modbus, PROFINET, EtherCAT, and OPC UA at line rate, a hardware root of trust, and keys derived from chip-level noise rather than stored secrets. The device operates from -40°C to 85°C.
  • Operational technology: PLCs, SCADA masters, and sensors on the plant floor.

The device has a physical write-lock switch that blocks network overwrites of its programming voltage. The write-lock allows the self-quarantine model to apply signed patches at every step, so no on-site step gates deployment. The AI never has direct write access to machinery.

Meeting SL-4 component requirements calls for a physical redesign on 5nm and 28nm process nodes. Three tiers use different nodes: 65/180nm for home and edge sensors, 28nm for SME and zone conduits, and 5nm FinFET for enterprise dark factories.

4. Market Context

The source cites a global shortage of roughly 33,000 open or implied FDE roles against fewer than 8,900 qualified engineers. It also cites a reported 84% failure rate for enterprise AI projects, attributed to legacy infrastructure and security gaps. These figures come from industry commentary and must be verified before external use.

The broader chip market is strong. SIA reports global chip sales of $791.7B in 2025, and projections for 2026 range from about $1 trillion to $1.5 trillion. Industrial chip market estimates for 2026 range from $63.5B to $95.1B. The conflicting estimates are a reason to cite a specific source and date for any market figure.

10. Deployment and Roadmap

Deployment. A standalone plant is staffed by a three-person team. A multi-site network is supported by central hub FDEs over 9 to 12 months. A fresh rollout plant takes 12 months across four phases. Deploying 80,000 factories over four years means about 20,000 new rollouts a year, each taking 12 months. Months 1 to 2 cover cloud ingestion and stress testing. Months 3 to 4 cover procurement, site preparation, and installing inline devices. Months 5 to 6 are a shadow-mode pilot that observes traffic without blocking it. Months 7 to 9 move all lines to live enforcement. Months 10 to 12 cover out-of-band patching and response drills.

Roadmap. Years 1 to 2 cover launch and certification (FIPS 140-3 and ISA/IEC 62443). Years 3 to 4 cover AI assurance, including signed data provenance and automated logging. Years 5 to 7 cover post-quantum migration of signing and key exchange, with each version recertified. Years 8 to 10 cover sustained lifecycle support, with end-of-life notice at least 24 months before the final update.

Shadow mode: the independent review recommends at least 60 days of shadow mode. The standard schedule provides about two months. The AI assurance timing in Years 3 to 4 is a change from the source's Years 3 to 5, and it must be confirmed.

11. Certification Status

SL-4 is a target, not a rating. Formal ISA/IEC 62443 and FIPS 140-3 testing is scheduled for Years 1 to 2. Client materials should describe the rating as a target until results are issued.

12. Workforce, Training, and Refund Exposure

The training program is a 12-month, online, credit-bearing Post-Graduate Advanced Diploma in Forward Deployed Engineering and Cyber-Physical Security, offered through University Visvesvaraya College of Engineering, Bangalore University. Admission uses a 150-minute, 100-mark assessment with a pass mark of 65, covering networking, applied AI, and a security scenario. Graduates progress from Level 1 (FDE Associate) to Level 4 (Global Principal FDE) based on field performance.

Training is delivered online, so the earlier model of 300 trainees per physical hub per year no longer applies. The base fee is ₹9.0 lakh for full operating cost, and ₹3.3 lakh is the instruction-only floor. No student discount applies. No fee has been approved. The model is self-funded, with a conditional repayment term that is pending legal review.

Refund exposure: under the proposed repayment term, each 300-student intake could owe up to ₹20.0 crore (about $2.38M) at full absorption. The aggregate depends on the intake size under the FDE ramp in Section 8, and no total is given here. That amount is not in any cash figure in this summary. Lab capital of ₹3.53 crore is also outside tuition and would need separate funding.

Absorption is conditional. It depends on clearing the assessment, the availability of Delper roles, and placement through the university. Only learners placed in Delper roles are absorbed. Learners placed with partner factories or Global Capability Centers become employees of those organizations.

13. Contract Terms

The Master Services Agreement (DELPER-MSA-2026-REV4) provides that Delper retains title to all Delper Guard hardware, which is leased to the customer rather than sold. Revenue is recognised over the subscription term, and Aegis owns the chips. Only anonymized threat signatures leave the plant. Delper issues signed patches for a registered zero-day as soon as they are ready, with no fixed delivery window. If fees remain unpaid 30 days after invoicing, cloud reasoning access is suspended, and the devices revert to a static, locally isolated state.

Employment status: resident FDEs are full employees of Delper Consulting, and the customer does not direct their employment. The agreement heading is aligned with this status, and the contracting party is Delper Consulting, the same entity as the employer.

17. Viability Analysis

On the Group's economics, Aegis is the more profitable route. Full-scale profit after tax is about $204B a year before SME and home service costs, against about $100B in the earlier model. The gap comes from the SME and home tiers, whose service cost is the main unknown.

Where the economics work

  • Enterprise and hardware: the subscription, markup and hardware-as-a-service give about $75B of revenue, or about $61B after tax, before SME and home costs.
  • Staffing at cost: the FDE pass-through recovers staffing cost, so the service does not lose money on people.
  • Lower customer price: the bill per line is about $95,000 a year, against about $258,000 earlier. The price is easier to accept.

Where the result depends on assumptions

Full-scale profit after tax falls to the earlier model's $100B if SME and home service costs reach about $126B a year before tax. That is about 73% of their $172.8B of revenue. Each $1 per SME per month of service cost is $0.34B a year before tax. Each $0.10 per home user per month is $7.2B a year before tax. The result is therefore very sensitive to these two costs, and neither is priced in the Group summary.

Funding and pool

Track 1 and Track 2 fund the build in Years 1 to 3, and from Year 11 Aegis profit services the bonds. The Group's pool model was built on the earlier Aegis profit, so it needs re-running on the new figures before the Year 27 clearance can be confirmed.

Verdict

Viable, subject to SME and home service costs. Enterprise and hardware are profitable on their own. The SME and home tiers carry most of the revenue, and the case rests on keeping their cost to serve below about $126B a year before tax. That cost has not been priced.

Tests before committing capital

  1. Price the cost to serve for the SME and home tiers, per user per month.
  2. Test the $95,000 per-line bill with five to ten prospective enterprise customers.
  3. Confirm the FDE ramp and set the training intake that delivers it.
  4. Re-run the Group's pool and debt schedule on the new Aegis profit.

These judgments rest on the Group summary's figures and this summary's calculations. The market statistics have not been verified, and this is not financial advice. An independent review of the pricing and cost assumptions should be done before any investment decision.

20. Autonomous Self-Quarantine Platform

Delper Guard can also act as the containment layer for the enterprise estate. A threat registry of 52 multi-domain baselines, mapped to CrowdStrike 2026 adversary behaviour, drives automated quarantine across identity, host, cloud, AI, edge and supply-chain boundaries. The goal is containment in under three seconds, because average eCrime breakout is now about 29 minutes and the fastest recorded case was 27 seconds. This section is drawn from a separate threat registry summary. The CrowdStrike figures are as quoted there and have not been independently verified.

Containment architecture

Telemetry is ingested and parsed in under one second. The SOAR engine matches each event to a registry baseline and fires the matching playbook. Containment then runs across six domains, covering all 52 registry threats.

Production requirements

  1. Telemetry latency. The SIEM or data lake must parse telemetry from chip registers, cloud control planes and endpoint sensors in under one second.
  2. Root-level API access. The containment engine would need persistent write access to identity providers, EDR controllers, cloud virtualisation planes and core network hardware, so it can inject defences without human authorisation.
  3. Context-aware safety interlocks. Before any high-impact containment script runs, playbooks must check cross-domain correlation. This prevents false positives from causing a self-inflicted outage, such as stopping a production database.
Unresolved conflict: requirement 2 calls for root-level write access to network hardware and other control planes. Section 2 states that the AI never has direct write access to machinery, and the device write-lock governs how patches are applied. The scope of autonomous write access needs an explicit decision before external use. The safety interlocks in requirement 3 should be specified and tested before any playbook acts without human approval.

21. Ransomware Resilience Features

This section adds the practices in Object First's Ransomware Survival Guide (2025) to the Aegis programme. The guide is written by a vendor of immutable backup storage, so its product claims are not adopted here. The operational practices are standard guidance, and they complement the containment layer in Section 20. Ransomware is treated as a case of when, not if, so each feature below is meant to run before an attack, during the first hours of one, and during recovery.

Prevention and hardening

Preventive controls from the guide.
ControlPractice
Zero Trust segmentationDivide the network into isolated zones so that an infection stays in one area, for example by separating user workstations from critical servers.
Least privilegeGrant users and systems only the minimum permissions their tasks need, which limits lateral movement after a compromise.
Multi-factor authentication and EDREnforce MFA and deploy endpoint detection and response. Secure and monitor RDP, VPN and third-party access, which are common entry points.
Patch managementKeep operating systems, applications and firmware current, prioritize critical patches, and keep an emergency rollout procedure for zero-day threats.
Email and user securityAuthenticate email with SPF, DKIM and DMARC, sandbox attachments, and run awareness training on phishing.
Backup strategyApply the 3-2-1 rule (three copies, two media types, one offsite or offline), run regular test restores, and use immutable storage.

Detection and first-hours response

Detection signals and response steps from the guide.
StagePractice
Early warning signsMass file renaming or encryption, sudden CPU or disk spikes, outbound connections to malicious hosts, and disabled antivirus or logging.
Centralized monitoringAggregate logs in a SIEM, combine alerts from antivirus, EDR, firewalls and intrusion detection, and map threats to MITRE ATT&CK.
IsolationIsolate affected systems and disconnect compromised devices, physically or by disabling switch ports.
Evidence preservationKeep RAM snapshots, disk images and logs. Do not delete files or power off systems unless incident response professionals advise it.
CommunicationUse channels that are not on the compromised network. Engage legal counsel, communications and law enforcement early, and notify the cyber insurer promptly.

Recovery and post-incident

Recovery and post-incident steps from the guide.
StagePractice
Root causeIdentify patient zero, the attack vector and lateral movement paths.

Integration note: the evidence and human-approval steps above apply to the containment platform in Section 20. Section 20 requirement 2 (root-level write access that acts without human authorisation) conflicts with the guide's emphasis on coordinated, human-led response, so the decision on autonomous write access should be made with this section in view.
