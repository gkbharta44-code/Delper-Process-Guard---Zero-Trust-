1. Purpose and Summary

Delper Aegis sells a hardware security device for factories, with a managed service that is billed separately. The device, Delper Guard, enforces security in hardware. A cloud AI platform and Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) run monitoring and plant-level operations. The program protects legacy operational technology (OT) from cyber threats while letting AI systems diagnose live plants.

This summary covers the product, the economics, the pricing structure, the staffing model, the training pipeline, the contract, the risks, and the decisions still open. Figures are from the source documents unless marked as an assumption or calculation. All years are program years, with program Year 1 as group Year 7, unless marked as group Year. Section 16 lists the items to resolve before external use.

2. Product and Architecture

  • Enterprise AI core: a cloud-hosted language model with a vector database of engineering documents, control programs, and incident history.
  • FDE platform: event pipelines, multi-agent orchestration, guardrails against prompt injection, and a service that retrieves data from the hardware.
  • Hardware (Delper Guard): inline deep packet inspection of Modbus, PROFINET, EtherCAT, and OPC UA at line rate, a hardware root of trust, and keys derived from chip-level noise rather than stored secrets. The device operates from -40°C to 85°C.
  • Operational technology: PLCs, SCADA masters, and sensors on the plant floor.

The device has a physical write-lock switch that blocks network overwrites of its programming voltage. The write-lock allows the self-quarantine model to apply signed patches at every step, so no on-site step gates deployment. The AI never has direct write access to machinery.

Meeting SL-4 component requirements calls for a physical redesign on 5nm and 28nm process nodes. Three tiers use different nodes: 65/180nm for home and edge sensors, 28nm for zone conduits, and 5nm FinFET for dark factories.

4. Market Context

The source cites a global shortage of roughly 33,000 open or implied FDE roles against fewer than 8,900 qualified engineers. It also cites a reported 84% failure rate for enterprise AI projects, attributed to legacy infrastructure and security gaps. These figures come from industry commentary and must be verified before external use.

The broader chip market is strong. SIA reports global chip sales of $791.7B in 2025, and projections for 2026 range from about $1 trillion to $1.5 trillion. Industrial chip market estimates for 2026 range from $63.5B to $95.1B. The conflicting estimates are a reason to cite a specific source and date for any market figure.

Note on the 33,000 figure: the market statistic and Delper's own active position count are different numbers and should not be presented as the same.

8. Staffing and FDE Coverage

Three FDEs are on duty at all times, one for each 8-hour shift, so 24/7 cover needs 1,095 shift-days a year per plant. Each FDE works five days a week. Covering 1,095 shift-days therefore needs 6.15 FDEs, rounded up to 7 per plant. Six would leave about 27 shifts a year uncovered.

The source projects $100.46B of enterprise annual recurring revenue at 1,000,000 lines and $2.01B from per-usage fees, totaling $102.47B at full scale. Restated for 960,000 lines, the platform fee is $96.44B and per-usage fees are about $1.93B, totaling about $98.37B. The $247.7B figure is a gross full-scale projection. It assumes the five-year plan of 960,000 lines is reached and staffed at 12 lines per plant, and that customers accept the total bill. 10. Deployment and Roadmap

Deployment. A standalone plant is staffed by a three-person team. A multi-site network takes three central hub FDEs plus one resident FDE per plant over 9 to 12 months. A fresh rollout plant takes 12 months across four phases. Deploying 80,000 factories over five years means about 16,000 new rollouts a year, each taking 12 months. Months 1 to 2 cover cloud ingestion and stress testing. Months 3 to 4 cover procurement, site preparation, and installing inline devices. Months 5 to 6 are a shadow-mode pilot that observes traffic without blocking it. Months 7 to 9 move all lines to live enforcement. Months 10 to 12 cover out-of-band patching and response drills.

Roadmap. Years 1 to 2 cover launch and certification (FIPS 140-3 and ISA/IEC 62443). Years 3 to 4 cover AI assurance, including signed data provenance and automated logging. Years 5 to 7 cover post-quantum migration of signing and key exchange, with each version recertified. Years 8 to 10 cover sustained lifecycle support, with end-of-life notice at least 24 months before the final update.

Shadow mode: the independent review recommends at least 60 days of shadow mode. The standard schedule provides about two months. The AI assurance timing in Years 3 to 4 is a change from the source's Years 3 to 5, and it must be confirmed.

12. Workforce, Training, and Refund Exposure

The training program is a 12-month, online, credit-bearing Post-Graduate Advanced Diploma in Forward Deployed Engineering and Cyber-Physical Security, offered through University Visvesvaraya College of Engineering, Bangalore University. Admission uses a 150-minute, 100-mark assessment with a pass mark of 65, covering networking, applied AI, and a security scenario. Graduates progress from Level 1 (FDE Associate) to Level 4 (Global Principal FDE) based on field performance.

The intake is 300 trainees per hub a year. The hub network is rebuilt to about 2,147 hubs at the Year 3 peak (Section 8), with a tiered fee structure from a 50% to a 10% discount. The base fee is ₹9.0 lakh for full operating cost, and ₹3.3 lakh is the instruction-only floor. No fee has been approved. The model is self-funded, with a conditional repayment term that is pending legal review. The "Corporate-Sponsored Paid/Reimbursable" label is not used, because it describes two different payers.

Refund exposure: under the proposed repayment term, each 300-student hub intake could owe up to ₹20.0 crore (about $2.38M) at full absorption. Across the 2,147 hubs at the Year 3 peak the total is about ₹42,940 crore, or about $5.1B. That amount is not in any cash figure in this summary, and it would reduce tuition revenue almost one-for-one. Lab capital of ₹3.53 crore is also outside tuition and would need separate funding.

Training is net of the student discount. Delper gives students a discount on the tuition, and the factory is charged the training fee for each FDE it takes. At the confirmed average discount of 30%, the factory fee of ₹9.0 lakh per placed FDE and the discount cost per trainee offset over five years, because each placed FDE needs about three trainees. Each 10 points of average discount moves the five-year net by about $0.7B, across about 2.1 million trainees.

The 16 to 20 week approval and procurement timeline applies to the upskilling track. The university route for freshers can take 6 to 12 months to approve.

Absorption is conditional. It depends on clearing the assessment, the availability of Delper roles, and placement through the university. Only learners placed in Delper roles are absorbed. Learners placed with partner factories or Global Capability Centers become employees of those organizations.

13. Contract Terms

The Master Services Agreement (DELPER-MSA-2026-REV4) provides that Delper retains title to all Delper Guard hardware, which is leased to the customer rather than sold. Revenue equals the unit price, recognised at deployment, and Aegis owns the chips. Only anonymized threat signatures leave the plant. Delper issues signed patches for a registered zero-day as soon as they are ready, with no fixed delivery window. If fees remain unpaid 30 days after invoicing, cloud reasoning access is suspended, and the devices revert to a static, locally isolated state.

Employment status: resident FDEs are full employees of Delper Consulting, and the customer does not direct their employment. The agreement heading is aligned with this status, and the contracting party is Delper Consulting, the same entity as the employer.

  • Customer acceptance: the monthly bill of about $21,501 per line is 157% above the source's $8,371.50 platform fee, which excludes the FDE.
  • Unpriced firmware cost: the $30.5M to $57.5M roadmap is not in the customer bill.
  • Certification: SL-4 is unconfirmed until testing completes in Years 1 to 2.
  • Wafer supply: the 5nm tier depends on advanced-node capacity, which the source does not address.
  • Academic timeline: university approval for freshers can take 6 to 12 months. The 16 to 20 week timeline applies to upskilling.
  • Home and student price path: a 10% annual rise takes the chip price to about USD 985 by program Year 25, and the segment implies about $1.25 trillion of revenue over 25 years.
  • Data sovereignty: routing threat data to regional databases and sharing GPU clusters between students and live operations require separation controls not yet specified.
  • Legal enforceability: repayment and retention terms vary by jurisdiction and need counsel review in each country.

17. Viability Analysis

The project is viable as a hardware business. As a managed service it depends on the hub network: the five-year plan needs about 560,000 FDEs by Year 5, matched only by growing the hubs from about 312 to about 2,147 by Year 3. The workforce no longer caps the line base, so the economics turn on density and on the price.

Where the economics work

  • Hardware margins: gross margin is 68% to 82% by tier, and gross profit before silicon amortization is $5,618.8M on $6,911.7M of revenue.
  • Staffing at cost: the FDE charge recovers staffing cost, so the service does not lose money on people.
  • Markup: the 20% markup is 20% of the FDE charge. At 12 lines per plant it adds $26,250 per line a year, or $25.20B across 960,000 lines.
  • Platform fee: the $8,371.50 monthly platform fee is the source's own subscription, worth about $96.44B a year across 960,000 lines.

Where the economics break

The binding constraint is now the hub build-out. Each plant needs 7 FDEs, so each line needs 0.583 FDEs at 12 lines per plant, and full scale at 960,000 lines needs 560,000 FDEs. Density still matters for price: the FDE charge is set at cost per line, so a denser plant lowers the bill per line.

Price risk

The total bill of $21,500.93 per line per month is 157% above the source's platform fee of $8,371.50, which excludes the FDE. Customers who accept only the platform fee would produce $96.44B at 960,000 lines, but the FDE staffing would then go unrecovered. The FDE charge falls as plants get denser, so the bill and the markup both depend on density. Acceptance of the full bill is the variable most likely to move the result, and it has not been tested with customers.

Funding risk

Launch costs are $1,824.3M before revenue, including $531.5M of silicon development, with a further $32.0M of AI capital spending in Year 1. Build-to-order reduces Year 1 funding to about $316.0M, against $1,544.2M under upfront production. GK Bharta advances the launch cost from its profit share, and Aegis repays the advance from its own profit as set out in Section 18. Cash turns positive in Year 2 under build-to-order and in Year 3 under upfront production, but only if the sales curve holds.

Costs outside the margin

Cloud hosting, firmware maintenance ($30.5M to $57.5M over ten years), hardware refresh, recruitment and training costs, refund exposure of up to ₹20.0 crore (about $2.38M) per 300-student hub intake, or about ₹42,940 crore ($5.1B) across the 2,147 hubs at the Year 3 peak, and overhead are not in the margin figures above. None of them changes the conclusion on its own, but together they reduce the margin materially.

Verdict

Conditionally viable. The hardware business is viable on its own. The five-year plan is staffed on schedule once the hub network is in place. Hubs are jurisdictional and carry no separate cost. At 12 lines per plant the recurring business is about $247.7B a year at the Section 6 price. The full-scale figure needs about 43 lines per plant, and at that density the FDE charge and markup per line fall, giving about $138.7B a year rather than $247.7B. Customers must also accept the 12-line bill of about $21,501 per line per month, and the launch must be funded through GK Bharta's advance. If any of these fails, the recurring figure does not hold.

Tests before committing capital

  1. Test the total bill with five to ten prospective customers, and record the number of lines each plant would contract.
  2. Survey plant sizes in the target segment. The 12-line base case may not be typical, and the FDE charge per line falls as plants get denser.
  3. Model a lighter coverage option, such as fewer FDEs on duty with stated response times, and compare its staffing cost and margin.
  4. Confirm the terms of GK Bharta's launch advance and repayment before committing to the 5nm tier.

These judgments rest on the source's figures and this summary's calculations. The market statistics have not been verified, and this is not financial advice. An independent review of the pricing and staffing assumptions should be done before any investment decision.

20. Self-Quarantine Software Specification

Self-quarantine is the software layer that detects, classifies, contains, verifies, and releases threats on top of the Delper Guard hardware described in Section 2. It maps 53 threats from the Splunk "Top 50 Cybersecurity Threats" contents page to the modules that detect and contain them. Threats 1 to 35 are checked against the full text. Threats 36 to 53 are provisional, taken from the contents page only.

20.1 Scope and Design Rule

This specification adds a self-quarantine capability to Aegis. A threat is detected, classified, contained, verified, and then released or escalated, with every step recorded in the sealed quarantine store. Containment is always a network, identity, or cloud action, or a safe-state action on the host. No containment step writes to a PLC or changes a control program, which preserves the hardware write-lock described in Section 2.

Safety rule for OT: any action that could change a physical process (isolating a controller, stopping a workload, cutting a link to a running line) runs only in a last-known-safe state and requires a human approval record, unless the safe-state rule in Section 20.3 applies.
Hardware boundary: the Delper Guard device can enforce only what crosses the plant network. Phishing, identity, SaaS, and cloud threats need connectors to the customer's identity provider, email and web gateways, endpoint tools, and cloud accounts. These connectors are software, and they are listed as required modules below. The summary does not currently price or scope them.

20.3 Quarantine State Machine

  1. Detect: QG-TI or QG-DPI raises a signal with a threat ID from the table in Section 20.4.
  2. Classify: QG-ORCH selects the playbook and confidence level. Low confidence records the event and does not contain.
  3. Contain: the playbook's actions run, scoped to the smallest affected unit (flow, session, host, account, workload, or segment). Safe-state rule: if an OT action could affect a running process, the Guard holds the last-known-safe state and waits for approval.
  4. Preserve: QG-SEAL records the evidence before any destructive step, including memory capture and configuration snapshots.
  5. Verify: QG-TI confirms the containment held, and QG-PV checks integrity of the affected component.
  6. Release or escalate: release on the playbook's condition, or hand off to a named human responder. Every transition is written to QG-SEAL.

Default timers: containment reviewed within one hour, release decisions within 24 hours, and a forced human review at 7 days for any quarantine still open. Automatic release never applies to insider, process-affecting, or legal-hold cases.
