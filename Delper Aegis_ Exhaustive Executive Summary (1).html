1. Purpose and Summary

Delper Aegis sells a hardware security device for factories, with a managed service that is billed separately. The device, Delper Guard, enforces security in hardware. A cloud AI platform and Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) run monitoring and plant-level operations. The program protects legacy operational technology (OT) from cyber threats while letting AI systems diagnose live plants.

This summary covers the product, the economics, the pricing structure, the staffing model, the training pipeline, the contract, the risks, and the decisions still open. Figures are from the source documents unless marked as an assumption or calculation. Section 16 lists the reconciliation points.

2. Product and Architecture

  • Enterprise AI core: a cloud-hosted language model with a vector database of engineering documents, control programs, and incident history.
  • FDE platform: event pipelines, multi-agent orchestration, guardrails against prompt injection, and a service that retrieves data from the hardware.
  • Hardware (Delper Guard): inline deep packet inspection of Modbus, PROFINET, EtherCAT, and OPC UA at line rate, a hardware root of trust, and keys derived from chip-level noise rather than stored secrets. The device operates from -40°C to 85°C.
  • Operational technology: PLCs, SCADA masters, and sensors on the plant floor.

The device has a physical write-lock switch that blocks network overwrites of its programming voltage. Signed patches pass the lock only through an on-site hardware security module. The AI never has direct write access to machinery.

Meeting SL-4 component requirements calls for a physical redesign on 5nm and 28nm process nodes. Three tiers use different nodes: 65/180nm for home and edge sensors, 28nm for zone conduits, and 5nm FinFET for dark factories.

4. Market Context

The source cites a global shortage of roughly 33,000 open or implied FDE roles against fewer than 8,900 qualified engineers. It also cites an 84% failure rate for enterprise AI projects. The source's figure matches a GFT Technologies survey (Wakefield Research, 2026), in which 84% of CIOs and CTOs said they had canceled at least one AI pilot or project because of legacy system limits. That is a cancellation rate, not a project failure rate. RAND Corporation reports that more than 80% of AI projects fail. The 33,000 FDE figure is confirmed. The figure of fewer than 8,900 qualified engineers remains unverified.

The broader chip market is strong. SIA reports global chip sales of $791.7B in 2025, and projections for 2026 range from about $1 trillion to $1.5 trillion. Industrial chip market estimates for 2026 range from $63.5B to $95.1B. The conflicting estimates are a reason to cite a specific source and date for any market figure.

Note on the 33,000 figure: 33,000 is the confirmed FDE figure. The market shortage above and the staffing requirement in Section 8 should both use this figure.

8. Staffing and FDE Coverage

A position that runs 24 hours a day, seven days a week requires 4.2 engineers under a 40-hour week (168 ÷ 40). A plant with full cover therefore needs 4.2 FDEs: 4 onsite and 0.2 FDE equivalent of online support. Three FDEs cover 120 of the 168 hours in a week (71%). The remaining 29% would have no resident engineer. The base case uses 4.2 FDEs per plant for Enterprise plants. SME plants receive online support only and add no resident FDEs.

10. Deployment and Roadmap

Deployment. A standalone plant takes a three-person team six months. A multi-site network takes three central hub FDEs plus one resident FDE per plant over 9 to 12 months. The standard rollout runs in four phases. Months 1 to 2 cover cloud ingestion and stress testing. Months 3 to 4 cover procurement, site preparation, and installing inline devices. Months 5 to 6 are a shadow-mode pilot that observes traffic without blocking it. Months 7 to 9 move all lines to live enforcement. Months 10 to 12 cover out-of-band patching and drills against the 72-hour window.

Roadmap. Years 1 to 2 cover launch and certification (FIPS 140-3 and ISA/IEC 62443). Years 3 to 5 cover AI assurance, including signed data provenance and automated logging. Years 6 to 7 cover post-quantum migration of signing and key exchange, with each version recertified. Years 8 to 10 cover sustained lifecycle support, with end-of-life notice at least 24 months before the final update.

Shadow mode: the independent review recommends at least 60 days of shadow mode. The standard schedule provides about two months. AI assurance runs Years 3 to 5, as in the source. AI assurance runs Years 3 to 5, and post-quantum migration starts in Year 6, so Year 5 is held by AI assurance only.

11. Certification Status

SL-4 is a target, not a rating. Formal ISA/IEC 62443 and FIPS 140-3 testing is scheduled for Years 1 to 2. Client materials should describe the rating as a target until results are issued.

12. Workforce, Training, and Refund Exposure

The training program has two tracks, both delivered as an online course through University Visvesvaraya College of Engineering, Bangalore University: a six-month upskilling course, and a 12-month full course, which is a credit-bearing Post-Graduate Advanced Diploma in Forward Deployed Engineering and Cyber-Physical Security. Admission uses a 150-minute, 100-mark assessment with a pass mark of 65, covering networking, applied AI, and a security scenario. Graduates progress from Level 1 (FDE Associate) to Level 4 (Global Principal FDE) based on field performance.

The intake is 300 students, with three hubs acting as single points of contact (SPOC) for students. The base fee is ₹9.0 lakh for full operating cost, with a single 10% discount, giving ₹8.1 lakh. No fee has been approved. The model is self-funded, with a 10% discount in place of any refund or repayment term. Jurisdiction legal review will be initiated upon deployment. The "Corporate-Sponsored Paid/Reimbursable" label is not used, because it describes two different payers.

Discount cost: the 10% discount on the ₹9.0 lakh fee reduces tuition by ₹0.9 lakh per student. Across 300 students, that is ₹2.7 crore (about $0.32M). No cash is refunded. Lab capital of ₹3.53 crore is retained for the practical component. It is outside tuition and will be funded in-house.

Absorption is conditional. It depends on clearing the assessment, the availability of Delper roles, and placement through the university. Only learners placed in Delper roles are absorbed. Learners placed with partner factories or Global Capability Centers become employees of those organizations.

13. Contract Terms

The Master Services Agreement (DELPER-MSA-2026-REV4) provides that Delper Consultants retains title to all Delper Guard hardware. Only anonymized threat signatures leave the plant. Delper guarantees a signed patch within 72 hours of a zero-day being triaged. If fees remain unpaid 30 days after invoicing, cloud reasoning access is suspended, and the devices revert to a static, locally isolated state.

Employment and contracting: Delper Consultants employs the resident FDEs and provides them to industrial customers on contract. The customer does not direct their employment. The heading "Independent Contractor Status" does not describe this arrangement and will be replaced before the agreement is shared. The agreement currently names Delper Technologies Corporation Ltd. as the contracting party. It must name Delper Consultants as the party providing FDE services before signature.

14. Principal Risks

  • Hiring capacity: the staffing plan requires more engineers than the source's talent figures suggest are available.
  • Customer acceptance: the monthly bill of about $15,707 per line is about 88% above the source's $8,371.50 subscription.
  • Firmware cost: the $30.5M to $57.5M roadmap is recovered through the platform fee, so margin depends on that fee.
  • Certification: SL-4 is unconfirmed until testing completes in Years 1 to 2.
  • Wafer supply: the 5nm tier depends on advanced-node capacity, which the source does not address.
  • Academic timeline: university approval can take 6 to 12 months. The 16 to 20 weeks in the source applies to upskilling existing FDEs, not to approval or procurement.
  • Operational access: the 72-hour patch commitment depends on physical access to the write-lock during maintenance windows.
  • Data sovereignty: routing threat data to regional databases and sharing GPU clusters between students and live operations require separation controls not yet specified.
  • Legal enforceability: retention terms vary by jurisdiction. Jurisdiction legal review will be initiated upon deployment.
  • Market statistics: the 84% figure is a cancellation rate from a GFT survey, not a failure rate. The 8,900 engineer figure is unverified.

17. Viability Analysis

The project is viable as a hardware business. It is viable as the managed service only if plant density, customer acceptance of the price, and funding all hold. Each condition is tested below.

Where the economics work

  • Hardware margins: gross margin is 68% to 82% by tier, and upfront contribution after silicon development is $5,351.5M on $7,250.0M of revenue.
  • Staffing at cost: the FDE charge recovers staffing cost, so the service does not lose money on people.
  • Markup: the 20% markup adds $15,750 per line a year, or about $15.75B across 1,000,000 lines. It does not depend on plant density.

Where the economics break

The binding constraint is workforce supply. The source's talent model supports 159,390 engineers in total. At 4.2 FDEs per plant, that covers about 37,950 plants, or about 26 lines per plant for 1,000,000 lines. The planning basis is 12 lines per plant, the confirmed figure.

Price risk

The total bill of $15,706.50 per line per month is 88% above the source's $8,371.50 subscription. If customers accept only the source's price, recurring revenue falls from about $188.5B to about $100.5B a year, roughly half. Price acceptance is the variable most likely to move the result, and it has not been tested with customers.

Funding risk

Launch costs are $1,898.5M before revenue, including $531.5M of silicon development. Build-to-order would reduce Year 1 funding to about $270.8M, but upfront production is selected, so Year 1 funding is $1,569.4M. Launch funding will be provided in-house. Cash turns positive in Year 2 under build-to-order and in Year 3 under upfront production, but only if the sales curve holds.

Costs outside the margin

Cloud hosting, firmware maintenance ($30.5M to $57.5M over ten years), hardware refresh, recruitment and training costs, the 10% fee discount (about $0.32M), and overhead are not in the margin figures above. None of them changes the conclusion on its own, but together they reduce the margin materially.

Verdict

Conditionally viable. The project is viable if three conditions hold together: plants average about 26 or more lines, customers accept a total bill near $15,707 per line per month, and the launch is funded. If any one fails, the $188.5B recurring revenue figure does not hold, and the project depends on the hardware business alone.

Tests before committing capital

  1. Test the total bill with five to ten prospective customers, and record the number of lines each plant would contract.
  2. Survey plant sizes in the target segment to confirm the 12-line planning basis.
  3. Model a lighter coverage option, such as remote response with stated response times, and compare its staffing cost and margin.
  4. Confirm that in-house funding covers the launch cost before committing to the 5nm tier.

These judgments rest on the source's figures and this summary's calculations. The market statistics have not been verified, and this is not financial advice. An independent review of the pricing and staffing assumptions should be done before any investment decision.
