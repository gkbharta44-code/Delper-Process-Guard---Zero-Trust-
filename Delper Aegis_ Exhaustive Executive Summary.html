1. Purpose and Summary

Delper Aegis sells a hardware security device for factories, with a managed service that is billed separately. The device, Delper Guard, enforces security in hardware. A cloud AI platform and Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) run monitoring and plant-level operations. The program protects legacy operational technology (OT) from cyber threats while letting AI systems diagnose live plants.

This summary covers the product, the economics, the pricing structure, the staffing model, the training pipeline, the contract, the risks, and the decisions still open. Figures are from the source documents unless marked as an assumption or calculation. Section 13 lists the reconciliation points.

2. Product and Architecture

  • Enterprise AI core: a cloud-hosted language model with a vector database of engineering documents, control programs, and incident history.
  • FDE platform: event pipelines, multi-agent orchestration, guardrails against prompt injection, and a service that retrieves data from the hardware.
  • Hardware (Delper Guard): inline deep packet inspection of Modbus, PROFINET, EtherCAT, and OPC UA at line rate, a hardware root of trust, and keys derived from chip-level noise rather than stored secrets. The device operates from -40°C to 85°C.
  • Operational technology: PLCs, SCADA masters, and sensors on the plant floor.

The device has a physical write-lock switch that blocks network overwrites of its programming voltage. Signed patches pass the lock only through an on-site hardware security module. The AI never has direct write access to machinery.

Meeting SL-4 component requirements calls for a physical redesign on 5nm and 28nm process nodes. Three tiers use different nodes: 65/180nm for home and edge sensors, 28nm for zone conduits, and 5nm FinFET for dark factories.

The device must withstand more than 5,000,000 malformed industrial frames per hour without failure. A dedicated security response team must issue a signed patch within 72 hours of triage. The write-lock requires physical access, so meeting that commitment depends on scheduled maintenance windows. The development process is designed to be auditable under IEC 62443-4-1, with signed commits and a hardware token for each developer.

4. Market Context

The source cites a global shortage of roughly 33,000 open or implied FDE roles against fewer than 8,900 qualified engineers. It also cites a reported 84% failure rate for enterprise AI projects, attributed to legacy infrastructure and security gaps. These figures come from industry commentary and must be verified before external use.

The broader chip market is strong. SIA reports global chip sales of $791.7B in 2025, and projections for 2026 range from about $1 trillion to $1.5 trillion. Industrial chip market estimates for 2026 range from $63.5B to $95.1B. The conflicting estimates are a reason to cite a specific source and date for any market figure.

Note on the 33,000 figure: the market statistic and Delper's own 33,000 active positions (Section 6) are different numbers and should not be presented as the same.

8. Staffing and FDE Coverage

A position that runs 24 hours a day, seven days a week requires 4.2 engineers under a 40-hour week (168 ÷ 40). A plant that needs full in-person cover therefore needs 4.2 FDEs. Three FDEs cover 120 of the 168 hours in a week (71%). The remaining 29% would have no resident engineer. The base case uses 4.2 FDEs per plant.

Under the base case, the FDE charge recovers staffing at cost, and the 20% markup is Delper's margin on staffing. The 350,000 FDEs are more than twice the 159,390 engineers the source's staffing model requires. Hiring capacity, not customer demand, is therefore the constraint on growth.

Talent supply

The source's supply model assumes 243 hubs, 300 graduates per hub per year (72,900 a year), and a 30% placement share into Delper roles. That gives 21,870 Delper hires a year. With 10% annual attrition, the workforce reaches the 159,390 target in Year 13. Without attrition, it reaches the target in Year 8. Attrition is the main reason the target is delayed. Placement share, not graduate supply, sets the pace.

Open point: the source describes both 300 graduates per batch and 300 per hub per year, and it uses both 81 and 243 hubs. These must be reconciled before headcount totals are final.

9. Recurring Revenue

The source projects $100.46B of enterprise annual recurring revenue and $2.01B from per-usage fees, totaling $102.47B at full scale. Under the current pricing, enterprise ARR at 1,000,000 lines is about $195.0B, including the FDE charge and markup.

The ARR figure is a gross projection. It assumes the full line base is reached, the coverage model is staffed, and customers accept the total bill. It does not account for the timing of growth, the recurring hardware and cloud costs, or the chip and firmware update cost in Section 6.

10. Deployment and Roadmap

Deployment. A standalone plant takes a three-person team six months. A multi-site network takes three central hub FDEs plus one resident FDE per plant over 9 to 12 months. The standard rollout runs in four phases. Months 1 to 2 cover cloud ingestion and stress testing. Months 3 to 4 cover procurement, site preparation, and installing inline devices. Months 5 to 6 are a shadow-mode pilot that observes traffic without blocking it. Months 7 to 9 move all lines to live enforcement. Months 10 to 12 cover out-of-band patching and drills against the 72-hour window.

Roadmap. Years 1 to 2 cover launch and certification (FIPS 140-3 and ISA/IEC 62443). Years 3 to 4 cover AI assurance, including signed data provenance and automated logging. Years 5 to 7 cover post-quantum migration of signing and key exchange, with each version recertified. Years 8 to 10 cover sustained lifecycle support, with end-of-life notice at least 24 months before the final update.

Shadow mode: the independent review recommends at least 60 days of shadow mode. The standard schedule provides about two months. The AI assurance timing in Years 3 to 4 is a change from the source's Years 3 to 5, and it must be confirmed.

11. Certification Status

SL-4 is a target, not a rating. Formal ISA/IEC 62443 and FIPS 140-3 testing is scheduled for Years 1 to 2. Client materials should describe the rating as a target until results are issued.

12. Workforce, Training, and Refund Exposure

The training program is a six-month, credit-bearing Post-Graduate Advanced Diploma in Forward Deployed Engineering and Cyber-Physical Security, offered through University Visvesvaraya College of Engineering, Bangalore University. Admission uses a 150-minute, 100-mark assessment with a pass mark of 65, covering networking, applied AI, and a security scenario. Graduates progress from Level 1 (FDE Associate) to Level 4 (Global Principal FDE) based on field performance.

The intake is 300 students across three hubs, with a tiered fee structure from a 50% to a 10% discount. The base fee is ₹9.0 lakh for full operating cost, and ₹3.3 lakh is the instruction-only floor. No fee has been approved. The model is self-funded, with a conditional repayment term that is pending legal review. The "Corporate-Sponsored Paid/Reimbursable" label is not used, because it describes two different payers.

Refund exposure: under the proposed repayment term, the 300-student intake could owe up to ₹20.0 crore (about $2.38M) at full absorption. That amount is not in any cash figure in this summary, and it would reduce tuition revenue almost one-for-one. Lab capital of ₹3.53 crore is also outside tuition and would need separate funding.

Absorption is conditional. It depends on clearing the assessment, the availability of Delper roles, and placement through the university. Only learners placed in Delper roles are absorbed. Learners placed with partner factories or Global Capability Centers become employees of those organizations.

13. Contract Terms

The Master Services Agreement (DELPER-MSA-2026-REV4) provides that Delper retains title to all Delper Guard hardware. Only anonymized threat signatures leave the plant. Delper guarantees a signed patch within 72 hours of a zero-day being registered. If fees remain unpaid 30 days after invoicing, cloud reasoning access is suspended, and the devices revert to a static, locally isolated state.

Employment status: resident FDEs are full employees of Delper Consulting, and the customer does not direct their employment. The heading "Independent Contractor Status" conflicts with this and must be replaced before the agreement is shared. The agreement names Delper Technologies Corporation Ltd. as the contracting party, so the employer and the contracting entity must be aligned.

14. Principal Risks

  • Hiring capacity: the staffing plan requires more engineers than the source's talent figures suggest are available.
  • Customer acceptance: the monthly bill of about $16,247 per line is nearly double the source's $8,371.50 subscription.
  • Unpriced firmware cost: the $30.5M to $57.5M roadmap is not in the customer bill.
  • Certification: SL-4 is unconfirmed until testing completes in Years 1 to 2.
  • Wafer supply: the 5nm tier depends on advanced-node capacity, which the source does not address.
  • Academic timeline: university approval can take 6 to 12 months, and the source recommends 16 to 20 weeks for approval and procurement.
  • Operational access: the 72-hour patch commitment depends on physical access to the write-lock during maintenance windows.
  • Data sovereignty: routing threat data to regional databases and sharing GPU clusters between students and live operations require separation controls not yet specified.
  • Legal enforceability: repayment and retention terms vary by jurisdiction and need counsel review in each country.
  • Market statistics: talent and failure-rate figures are unverified.

17. Viability Analysis

The project is viable as a hardware business. It is viable as the managed service only if plant density, customer acceptance of the price, and funding all hold. Each condition is tested below.

Where the economics work

  • Hardware margins: gross margin is 68% to 82% by tier, and upfront contribution after silicon development is $5,351.0M on $7,250.0M of revenue.
  • Staffing at cost: the FDE charge recovers staffing cost, so the service does not lose money on people.
  • Markup: the 20% markup adds $15,750 per line a year, or about $15.75B across 1,000,000 lines. It does not depend on plant density.

Where the economics break

The binding constraint is workforce supply. The source's talent model supports 159,390 engineers in total. At 4.2 FDEs per plant, that covers about 37,950 plants, or about 26 lines per plant for 1,000,000 lines. The pilot shows 12 lines per plant.

Price risk

The total bill of $16,246.50 per line per month is 94% above the source's $8,371.50 subscription. If customers accept only the source's price, recurring revenue falls from about $195.0B to about $100.5B a year, roughly half. Price acceptance is the variable most likely to move the result, and it has not been tested with customers.

Funding risk

Launch costs are $1,899.0M before revenue, including $531.5M of silicon development. Build-to-order reduces Year 1 funding to about $270.8M. The source does not name a funding source for either approach. Cash turns positive in Year 2 under build-to-order and in Year 3 under upfront production, but only if the sales curve holds.

Costs outside the margin

Cloud hosting, firmware maintenance ($30.5M to $57.5M over ten years), hardware refresh, recruitment and training costs, refund exposure of up to ₹20.0 crore (about $2.38M), and overhead are not in the margin figures above. None of them changes the conclusion on its own, but together they reduce the margin materially.

Verdict

Conditionally viable. The project is viable if three conditions hold together: plants average about 26 or more lines, customers accept a total bill near $16,247 per line per month, and the launch is funded. If any one fails, the $195.0B recurring revenue figure does not hold, and the project depends on the hardware business alone.

Tests before committing capital

  1. Test the total bill with five to ten prospective customers, and record the number of lines each plant would contract.
  2. Survey plant sizes in the target segment. The 12-line pilot may not be typical.
  3. Model a lighter coverage option, such as remote response with stated response times, and compare its staffing cost and margin.
  4. Identify a funding source for the launch cost before committing to the 5nm tier.

These judgments rest on the source's figures and this summary's calculations. The market statistics have not been verified, and this is not financial advice. An independent review of the pricing and staffing assumptions should be done before any investment decision.
