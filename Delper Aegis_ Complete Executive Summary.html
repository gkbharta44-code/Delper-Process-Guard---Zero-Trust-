1. Overview

Delper Aegis combines a hardware security product with a managed service. Delper Guard, a ruggedized DIN-rail inline firewall and transaction security coprocessor, enforces security in silicon. A cloud-hybrid AI platform and a team of Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) handle diagnostics, monitoring, and plant-level operations. The goal is to protect legacy factory operational technology (OT) from cyber threats while letting AI systems diagnose problems in live plants.

The program is built on separation of concerns. Delper Guard holds the physical enforcement boundary, and the AI never has direct write access to machinery. Delper Guard has moved from a consumer USB endpoint key to an industrial product, and it targets the SL-4 level, which is designed to resist attackers with substantial resources and specialist skills.

The program targets four failure modes specific to autonomous factories:

Telemetry injection
Blocks artificial parameters fed into unencrypted IIoT sensors that would blind anomaly detection.
Digital twin hijacking
Stops lateral movement from compromised IT systems into operational virtual replicas.
Kinematic tampering
Rejects microcode or G-code changes that could make machines produce parts with silent structural defects.
Autonomy ransomware cascades
Ensures network deadlocks or account changes do not halt the production line.

2. Four-Layer Architecture

Layer 1: Enterprise AI Reasoning Core

A cloud-hosted large language model (such as Llama-3-70B-Instruct) with a pgvector database of engineering documentation, G-code blueprints, and incident history.

Layer 2: FDE Platform and Orchestration

Kafka event pipelines, LangGraph multi-agent orchestration, NVIDIA NeMo guardrails against prompt injection, and a Go-based Vault Conduit service that retrieves data from the hardware.

Layer 3: Hardware Security (Delper Guard)

An inline DIN-rail device that performs deep packet inspection on Modbus, PROFINET, EtherCAT, and OPC UA traffic at line rate. It runs a quantized triage model and uses physically unclonable functions (PUFs) to derive startup keys from SRAM noise.

Layer 4: Operational Technology

PLCs, SCADA masters, and IIoT sensors on the plant floor.

3. Hardware and Silicon

Meeting SL-4 component requirements calls for a full physical redesign on 5nm and 28nm process nodes. The core capabilities are:

Inline packet filtering

Parses L2 and L3 frames at line rate.

Deep packet inspection

Hardware coprocessors decode industrial protocols in isolated trenches.

Flashable secure boot

A hardware root of trust accepts signed, rollback-protected updates over a 10-year life.

Physically unclonable functions

On-chip SRAM noise yields authentication keys at startup, with no static secrets in flash.

Enclosure and write protection

The DIN-rail enclosure operates from -40°C to 85°C and offers dual or quad SFP and RJ-45 Gigabit ports. A hardware-isolated switch layer and a mechanical fail-to-wire relay prevent line locks.

A physical SPDT switch sits inline with the gate of a P-channel MOSFET. In Lock mode it grounds the gate and cuts the programming voltage (VPP), so flash cannot be rewritten over the network. Signed patches pass the lock only through a hardware-validated path from the on-site hardware security module (HSM).

4. Secure Development and Threat Model

The development process is designed to be auditable under IEC 62443-4-1. Every commit to the Verilog parser or HSM firmware passes an automated CI/CD gate and must carry a signature tied to a verified developer hardware token. Firmware follows MISRA C:2012, and hardware description code must pass linting for timing race hazards.

The device must withstand more than 5,000,000 malformed Modbus, PROFINET, and EtherCAT frames per hour without memory leaks, lockups, or relay drops. A dedicated Product Security Incident Response Team (PSIRT) must sign and distribute a rollback-protected patch within 72 hours of triage.

7. Upskilling Program (UVCE)

The program is a six-month, credit-bearing Post-Graduate Advanced Diploma in Forward Deployed Engineering and Cyber-Physical Security, hosted by the Computer Science department at University Visvesvaraya College of Engineering (UVCE), Bangalore University. Cohorts draw from final-year CSE, ISE, and ECE students and working professionals.

  • Months 1–2: Lab commissioning and board approval.
  • Months 3–4: Foundational bootcamps in deep packet inspection, packet capture analysis, Kafka, and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG).
  • Month 5: Advanced labs in LangGraph, adversarial fuzzing of up to 5,000,000 malformed frames per hour, PUF handshakes, and IEC 62443-4-1 secure coding.
  • Month 6: A capstone building a shadow-mode digital twin with a partner factory, followed by placement through the UVCE Placement Cell into Bengaluru's Global Capability Centers (GCCs).

Facilities: Edge hardware sits in the MARVEL R&D Lab. A six-GPU NVIDIA A100 cluster sits in the UVCE IT Centre and is accessed remotely through K3s.

Software stack: LangGraph, LlamaIndex, pgvector, Hugging Face Transformers, Apache Kafka, Go, Docker, K3s, Wireshark, Scapy, OpenPLC, MISRA C:2012 linters, Verilog linting tools, and HashiCorp Vault. The two-week pre-launch window covers racking hardware, installing Delper Guard boards inline between PLC simulators and attack injectors, and validating the gateway integration.

10. Autonomous Triage and Self-Healing

Human response is removed from the critical path. A hardware-isolated routine runs quantized ML models, scaling from 0.1 million parameters on edge IoT devices to 100 million parameters on the enterprise 5nm tier, which uses an external DRAM interface.

Suspicious packets and unauthorized commands are intercepted in real time, and the affected line is isolated. Activity is written to a sealed Quarantine Vault rather than erased, which keeps the audit trail intact for FIPS 140-3 and PCI-DSS verification. When host software or drivers drift from normal behavior, pre-approved encrypted fixes are applied from a secure whitelist, so transaction processing continues.

11. Commercial Model

Delper positions itself as a Hardware-Enabled Managed Service provider, selling "Aegis-Factory as a Service" rather than standalone chips. The model combines three components: the Delper Guard hardware (the shield), trained FDEs embedded on site (the engine), and the cloud-hybrid multi-agent platform (the brain). The proposed pricing is:

  • Upfront: $6,500 per production line, covering hardware provisioning and site wiring.
  • Monthly: $8,371.50, covering cloud orchestrator access, threat index updates, and one resident FDE assigned to the plant.

The value proposition is an IEC 62443 SL-4 security rating and a self-healing factory. The source also projects a $102.47 billion annual recurring revenue opportunity at full scale, which is not attributed to a specific study.

12. Deployment Model and Rollout

  • Standalone plant: A three-person pod (AI core, OT data pipeline, SecOps hardware) over six months.
  • Multi-site network: Three central hub FDEs plus one resident FDE per plant, over 9 to 12 months.

Twelve-month rollout

  1. Months 1–2Core cloud ingestion. Schema deployment, Kafka linkage, and stress testing of the central stack.
  2. Months 3–4Procurement and site preparation. Ship hardware and install inline appliances at plants.
  3. Months 5–6Shadow-mode pilot. Non-blocking operation across all sites. Edge models are tuned and safe protocol behaviors are whitelisted.
  4. Months 7–9Live enforcement. All lines move to live blocking.
  5. Months 10–12Out-of-band patching. Deploy the HSM network, formalize dual-custody procedures, and run patch drills against the 72-hour window.

14. Contractual Terms

The Master Services Agreement (DELPER-MSA-2026-REV4) provides the following:

  • Delper retains title to all Delper Guard hardware.
  • The customer grants Delper personnel facility access to toggle the write-lock inside electrical cabinets during scheduled maintenance windows.
  • Resident FDEs remain full employees of Delper Consulting. The customer provides workspace, network access, and plant safety protocols but does not direct their employment.
  • Only anonymized threat signatures leave the plant, under a Semantic Scrubbing guarantee.
  • Delper guarantees a signed, rollback-protected patch within 72 hours of a zero-day being registered with its PSIRT.
  • If fees remain unpaid 30 days after invoicing, cloud reasoning access is suspended. Delper Guard units revert to a static, locally isolated state, and autonomous updates pause.

16. Candidate Assessment

The assessment runs 150 minutes for 100 marks, with a passing mark of 65. It has three sections:

  • Networks and protocols (30 marks): Modbus TCP frame structure, placement of deep packet inspection, and a Kafka consumer that scrubs sensitive metadata before cloud routing.
  • Applied AI (40 marks): A LangGraph state graph with conditional routing, plus a pgvector chunking strategy and a cosine-distance similarity query.
  • Security scenario (30 marks): A step-by-step procedure for delivering an emergency patch to a device whose write-lock is engaged.

19. Overall Assessment

Strengths: Clear separation between hardware enforcement and software reasoning; a physics-based security model; a credible talent pipeline through a public university; a coherent financial structure; and a recurring-revenue service model.

Key considerations for execution:

  • Budget scenarios should be consolidated into a single approved figure.
  • Market and performance statistics should be validated against primary sources.
  • The 72-hour patch commitment depends on dual-custody staffing and scheduled maintenance windows, given the physical write-lock design.
  • Academic approval timelines should allow for 8 to 12 weeks or more of additional lead time.
  • The 24-week training window is best suited to candidates with prior embedded or systems experience.
  • Shadow mode should run for at least 60 days before live enforcement.
  • SL-4 certification should be presented as a target until formal testing is complete.

Bottom line: The architecture and business model are sound and differentiated. Successful execution depends on aligning the university and procurement timelines, consolidating the budget, setting firm candidate entry requirements, completing certification, and putting the operational safeguards described above in place.
